On Friday, August 28, colleagues, former students, and friends gathered at McGill University to honour the life, intellect, and enduring legacy of Professor Michael P. Païdoussis.

![The Journal of Fluids and Structures, Volume 1, Number 2 (April 1987), the memorial card and the programme of the tribute to Professor Michael P. Païdoussis](/uploads/2026/08/paidoussis-tribute-1-memorial.jpg)

Prof. Païdoussis, my MSc supervisor, was one of the founding architects of modern fluid-structure interactions. He was an erudite scholar who travelled the world, but above all, a true gentleman of immense human qualities. As a mentor and journal editor, his meticulous attention to detail was legendary; many of us remember drafts returned generously covered in red ink, each mark a testament to the high standards and intellectual rigour he instilled in everyone he supervised.

He transmitted an infectious passion for curiosity-driven research. Whether uncovering the nonlinear dynamics of pipes conveying fluid, the flutter of flags, rods and shells, or the complex dynamics of cylinder arrays in cross-flow, his work laid the theoretical and experimental foundations upon which our community continues to build.

The memorial event was a moving occasion to reconnect with long-time colleagues and former professors, to reflect on his foundational treatises, and to show my own students the lineage of scientific inquiry they now inherit.

![Presentation at the tribute, "Flags and Pipes as Model Systems for Flow-induced Vibrations in Hydraulic Turbines"](/uploads/2026/08/paidoussis-tribute-2-talk.jpg)
